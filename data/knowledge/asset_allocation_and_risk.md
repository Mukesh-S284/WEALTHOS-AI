# Asset Allocation, Volatility, and Portfolio Diversification

## Modern Portfolio Theory and Diversification
Asset allocation refers to the distribution of an investment portfolio across diverse asset categories: Equities, Fixed Income (Bonds), Cash Equivalents, Gold/Commodities, and Alternative Assets.
Nobel laureate Harry Markowitz demonstrated that combining non-correlated or negatively correlated assets reduces overall portfolio volatility (standard deviation) without sacrificing expected long-term return.

## Understanding Historical Drawdown and Volatility
Volatility is commonly measured as the annualized standard deviation of daily logarithmic returns, scaled by the square root of 252 trading days.
Maximum Drawdown (MDD) measures the peak-to-trough decline during a specific period.
For example, during the 2008 Global Financial Crisis, the S&P 500 experienced a maximum drawdown of approximately -56%, while broad investment-grade US bonds (BND/AGG) gained over +5%, highlighting the stabilizing power of multi-asset allocation.
Concentration risk arises when a portfolio is heavily weighted in a single stock, sector, or asset class (measured by the Herfindahl-Hirschman Index).
