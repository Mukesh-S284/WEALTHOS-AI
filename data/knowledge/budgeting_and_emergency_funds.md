# Personal Budgeting and Emergency Fund Foundations
Publisher: National Financial Education Standards / FINRA Guidelines
Date: 2026-02-01
Category: Financial Planning

## The 50/30/20 Budgeting Framework
A widely recognized guideline for household budgeting:
- 50% for Needs: Essential living expenses including rent or mortgage, utilities, basic groceries, healthcare, minimum debt obligations, and necessary transit.
- 30% for Wants: Discretionary expenses including dining out, entertainment, vacations, electronics, and luxury purchases.
- 20% for Savings and Debt Acceleration: Contributions toward emergency funds, retirement plans, long-term savings goals, and extra debt principal payments.

## Emergency Fund Sizing
An emergency fund is a liquid cash reserve kept in a high-yield savings account (HYSA) or money market instrument.
Standard recommendations:
- 3 to 6 months of essential living expenses for dual-income households with stable employment.
- 6 to 12 months for single-income households, freelancers, self-employed individuals, or those with variable commission-based compensation.
An emergency fund prevents an investor from being forced to liquidate long-term equity or mutual fund holdings at depressed prices during unexpected life shocks.
